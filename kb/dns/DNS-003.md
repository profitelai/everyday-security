# DNS-003 · Domain auto-renew is on and expiry is monitored

## Why it matters
An expired domain stops resolving, takes web and mail down with it, and after the registration lapses it can pass through grace and redemption stages that cost extra or, eventually, be released for anyone to register. Most expiries are caused by a lapsed card or reminder emails nobody reads, not by neglect of the domain itself.

## How to test
```bash
whois example.com | grep -i -E 'expir'
```
The field name varies by registry (for example "Registry Expiry Date"). Then in the registrar panel confirm that auto-renew is on, the payment method on file is valid beyond the next renewal date, and the renewal-reminder address is a mailbox someone reads that is not hosted on the domain itself.

## Pass criteria
Auto-renew is on, the stored payment method will not expire before the renewal, reminders reach a monitored mailbox, and an independent reminder exists (calendar entry or a scheduled script that alerts when the expiry date is under about 60 days away).

## Fix
Enable auto-renew, update the payment method, and register the domain for several years where the budget allows. Add a recurring alert that reads the expiry date, for example a weekly job that runs the whois command above and warns on short remaining time. Keep one list of all owned domains and who pays for each.

## Risk and rollback
Auto-renew will also renew domains you meant to drop, so keep the domain list current and switch auto-renew off for those you are retiring on purpose. Rollback is switching the setting off per domain; renewals already charged follow the registrar's refund terms.

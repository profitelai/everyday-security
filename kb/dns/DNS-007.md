# DNS-007 · No CNAME or delegation points at a deprovisioned service (subdomain takeover review)

## Why it matters
When a site, bucket or app on a cloud platform is deleted but the CNAME that pointed to it stays, someone else can sometimes claim that platform name and serve content under your subdomain. That content appears under your own domain, so visitors trust it, and it can be used for phishing or to read cookies scoped to the parent domain. The same applies to NS records delegating a subzone to a provider where the zone no longer exists.

## How to test
Export the zone (provider panel, API, or zone file) into `names.txt`, one hostname per line.
```bash
while read -r h; do
  t=$(dig +short CNAME "$h" | head -1)
  [ -z "$t" ] && continue
  s=$(dig +noall +comments "$t" A | grep -o 'status: [A-Z]*')
  echo "$h -> $t ($s)"
done < names.txt
```
Investigate every target that returns `NXDOMAIN`. For targets that still resolve, request the page (`curl -sI https://host`) and look for a provider's "no such app/site/bucket" message, which signals an unclaimed resource.

## Pass criteria
Each CNAME target resolves and serves a resource you control, and no NS delegation points at a zone that does not exist. Records for retired services have been deleted.

## Fix
Delete the DNS record, or reclaim the resource on the provider. Make record removal the first step of every decommission, ahead of deleting the cloud resource, and add a DNS cleanup line to the offboarding checklist. Re-run this review after each large migration.

## Risk and rollback
Deleting a record that something still uses breaks that hostname. Check access logs and ask the owning team first, and keep a zone export from before the change. Rollback: re-create the record from that export.

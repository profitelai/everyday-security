# PRIV-006 · A confidentiality-incident register records every incident and is kept for five years

## Why it matters
Every business under the Quebec Act must keep a register of confidentiality incidents and give the Commission a copy on request (s. 3.8). An incident is any access, use or communication not authorized by law, a loss, or any other breach of protection (s. 3.6). The Commission stresses the register includes incidents that carry no serious-injury risk. The regulation on confidentiality incidents lists what each entry holds: description of the information, brief circumstances, when it happened, when you found out, number of people (or an estimate), the reasoning behind your risk conclusion, notice dates and any public notice with reasons, and the measures taken. Entries are kept up to date and for at least five years from when you became aware (Reg. ss. 7 and 8). PIPEDA s. 10.3 has its own record duty for security-safeguard breaches. This is operational guidance, not legal advice; confirm obligations for your situation with a qualified lawyer.

## How to test
1. Ask to see the register and where it lives. Check access is limited and a backup exists.
2. Compare its columns to the list above; each of the eight elements should have a place.
3. Read the entries. A register that holds only large breaches, or nothing at all after years, suggests staff do not report small events (misdirected email, lost phone, wrong attachment). Ask two staff what they would do.
4. Check the oldest entry is still present and that incidents at providers holding your data are logged too, since you stay responsible for them.

## Pass criteria
A register exists in a controlled place with all eight elements. Entries are complete and dated, include non-notifiable incidents and provider-side incidents, and are retained at least five years after awareness. A copy can be produced quickly.

## Fix
Start from a simple spreadsheet or form with the eight fields as mandatory columns. Add a one-line instruction for staff to report anything odd to the person in charge. Record the decision and reasoning even when you conclude there is no serious risk.

## Risk and rollback
The register itself holds sensitive detail; restrict it and avoid pasting the leaked personal data into it, describe it instead. Backdated entries damage credibility, so write entries when found and amend with dated notes. Rollback: if a field was missed, add a dated correction rather than overwriting.

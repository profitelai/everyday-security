# VEN-002 · Critical vendors provide security evidence proportionate to their tier, and the evidence is read

## Why it matters
A vendor saying "we take security seriously" proves nothing. Independent reports (a SOC 2 report from a CPA firm, or an ISO/IEC 27001 certificate from an accredited certification body) show that someone outside the vendor looked at its controls. They are only useful if you check what they cover and what they found. A small business cannot send a 300-question form to every supplier and should not try. The Cyber Centre suggests using third-party assessments for critical suppliers and re-evaluating suppliers regularly, which means spending effort where the tier from VEN-001 says it matters.

## How to test
1. From the inventory, take every tier 1 vendor and a sample of tier 2. For each, find the evidence on file and its date.
2. For a SOC 2 report, open it and check: the report type (Type 2 covers a period of operation, Type 1 only a point in time); the period ends within roughly the last 12 months, or a bridge letter covers the gap; the scope names the service you actually use; the trust criteria include Security (and Confidentiality or Privacy if you send such data); the auditor's opinion is unmodified; any listed exceptions have a management response; and the complementary user entity controls (things the report expects you to do, such as enabling MFA) are noted and done.
3. For an ISO/IEC 27001 certificate, check the certificate has not expired, the certification body is accredited, the scope statement covers your service and location, and ask for the Statement of Applicability if the decision is important.
4. For tier 2 and 3 vendors with no report, look for a public security or trust page, support for MFA and single sign-on, a named security contact, a breach history, and a short written answer to a handful of questions (where is data stored, who can access it, is it encrypted, how fast will you tell us about an incident, can we export and delete our data).
5. Reports are normally under NDA. Check they are stored with restricted access.

## Pass criteria
Each tier 1 vendor has current, relevant evidence on file with a note of who read it and what follow-up was needed (for example enabling MFA on your side). Lower tiers have the short questions answered or a documented reason for accepting the risk. Evidence is refreshed at least yearly for tier 1 and when the contract renews.

## Fix
Keep a one-page questionnaire of 8 to 12 questions and use it only where no report exists. Ask the vendor sales or security contact for the latest report under NDA. If a critical vendor has nothing, record the risk with an owner and a date, ask for a plan, and consider switching at renewal. Note control gaps on your side (such as MFA not enabled) and fix them.

## Risk and rollback
Asking for evidence can slow onboarding, and some small vendors will have no reports; that is a risk decision, not automatically a reason to refuse. A report does not prove the service is secure, only what was tested in a stated period. Rollback: if a request stalls a project, apply compensating controls such as sending the vendor less data, and revisit at renewal.

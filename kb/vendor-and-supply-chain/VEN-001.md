# VEN-001 · A vendor inventory records what data and access each supplier has, and ranks them by risk

## Why it matters
A small business usually runs on dozens of outside services: hosting, email, payments, accounting, CRM, analytics, chat widgets, an agency or freelancer with admin rights. An attacker who cannot get to you directly can often get to one of them, and you cannot protect or end a relationship you have forgotten about. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security advises starting with a list of every third party that touches your organization and sorting them by how critical they are, because not all vendors deserve the same effort. Under PIPEDA you stay accountable for personal information that a vendor processes for you (see the OPC guidance on processing across borders), so the list is also the starting point for PRIV-004 and PRIV-009.

## How to test
1. Build the list from evidence, not memory. Sources: the accounts-payable ledger and card statements for the last 12 months; the "Sign in with" and connected-apps pages of your main Google, Microsoft or other single sign-on account; the domain's records, which often reveal SaaS tools.
```bash
dig +short TXT example.com | tr ' ' '\n' | grep -E 'include:|verification|site-verification'
dig +short MX example.com
```
   Also list who has a login to your hosting, registrar, source repository and website admin.
2. Open the inventory (a spreadsheet is enough) and check that every row has: vendor and service; business owner; what data it holds (none, business, personal, payment, credentials); what access it has to your systems (none, read, write, admin; accounts, API keys, OAuth grants); where the data is stored if known; contract or terms location; renewal date; risk tier (for example 1 critical, 2 important, 3 minor).
3. Spot check three vendors from the statements that are not on the list. Each missing one is a finding.
4. Check that tier is set by what happens if the vendor is breached or disappears (data exposed, access gained, business stops), not by contract size.

## Pass criteria
Every vendor with access to your data or systems is listed with an owner, the data and access it has, and a tier. The list was reviewed in the last 12 months. Spot checks find no unlisted vendor. Vendors holding personal data are flagged and link to the register kept for PRIV-004.

## Fix
Create the spreadsheet, fill it from the statements and account lists, and give each vendor one named owner inside the business. Add a step to buying and onboarding: no new tool receives customer data or admin access until it is on the list. Remove accounts and OAuth grants for tools you no longer use and cancel their subscriptions. Review the list once a year and whenever someone leaves.

## Risk and rollback
The main cost is time. Revoking unused OAuth grants or accounts can break an integration someone still relies on, so ask the owner first and note what you revoked. Rollback: re-authorize the connection or restore the account from the vendor's console.

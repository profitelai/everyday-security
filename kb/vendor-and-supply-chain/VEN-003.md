# VEN-003 · Vendor contracts state breach notice, data location, sub-processors and exit terms

## Why it matters
When a vendor is breached or fails, the contract decides whether you hear about it in hours or in a news story, whether your data is in a country you did not expect, and whether you can get it back. The Cyber Centre recommends putting a notification time frame for incidents and vulnerabilities into supplier contracts. Under PIPEDA you remain accountable for personal information handled by a processor, and the Privacy Commissioner expects contractual or other means of comparable protection. PRIV-009 covers the privacy elements that Quebec law requires in writing for providers handling personal information. This check is wider: it covers business data too, and the operational terms that decide how a bad day plays out. This is operational guidance, not legal advice; have a lawyer review contracts that matter.

## How to test
For each tier 1 and tier 2 vendor from VEN-001, open the contract, terms of service and any data-processing addendum, and mark each item found, missing or unclear.
1. Incident notice: the vendor must tell you of a security incident affecting your data within a stated time (a number of hours or days, not "promptly" alone), to a named contact, with the facts you need to assess your own duties.
2. Data location: where your data is stored and processed (countries or regions), and whether that can change without notice.
3. Sub-processors: the vendor lists the other companies that handle your data, tells you before adding one, and gives you a way to object.
4. Security commitments: a minimum standard (encryption, access control, MFA for the vendor's staff, backups), and a promise to give you evidence (VEN-002) on request.
5. Exit: how long you can export your data after notice, in what format, at what cost, when the vendor deletes it afterwards and whether it confirms deletion in writing; and what happens on insolvency or acquisition.
6. Service levels, liability caps and insurance: note them so the cost of a failure is known in advance.
7. Changes: the vendor must give notice of material changes to terms or services.

## Pass criteria
Every tier 1 vendor has a contract or addendum covering items 1 to 5 in writing, or the gap is recorded with an owner and a decision (accept, negotiate, or replace). Tier 2 vendors on standard online terms have been read once for items 1, 2 and 5. The contract file for each vendor is stored with the inventory row.

## Fix
Ask each vendor for its data-processing addendum and sub-processor list; most SaaS vendors have standard ones. For local providers, use a short addendum that names the breach-notice period, data location, sub-processor rule and return-and-delete terms. Where a large vendor will not negotiate, limit what you send it, enable the protective settings it offers, and keep your own copy of the data (VEN-010).

## Risk and rollback
Pressing for terms can slow a purchase, and small vendors may refuse bespoke clauses; the alternative is to reduce the data and access they get. Do not copy clauses from another company's contract without legal advice. Rollback: if a negotiation fails, continue under existing terms, record the accepted risk, and plan the exit.

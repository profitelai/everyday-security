# VEN-004 · Someone is told when a critical vendor reports a security incident or an exploited flaw

## Why it matters
Vendors rarely phone you. Incident notices appear on a status page, a security bulletin or a customer email that lands in a shared inbox nobody reads. The delay between the vendor knowing and you acting is the time attackers use your credentials, tokens and exposed integrations. CISA publishes a catalog of vulnerabilities known to be exploited in the wild, which is a reliable short list of flaws to treat as urgent for any product you run yourself. Monitoring is cheap: it is mostly subscriptions and one named person.

## How to test
1. For each tier 1 vendor from VEN-001, ask the owner to show how they would learn of a vendor incident: a subscription to the vendor status page or security bulletin feed, a vendor email address that reaches a monitored mailbox, and your contact details in the vendor's security-contact field.
2. Check that vendor mail is not sent only to one person's personal inbox or to a former employee. Look for a shared alias such as security@ or it@ and confirm who reads it.
3. For software you host yourself (CMS, plugins, server software, appliances), compare the product list with the catalog of known exploited vulnerabilities:
```bash
curl -s https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json \
  | jq -r '.vulnerabilities[] | select((.vendorProject+" "+.product) | test("wordpress|nginx|openssl|microsoft"; "i")) | [.dateAdded,.cveID,.vendorProject,.product] | @tsv' | tail -20
```
   Replace the pattern with your own vendors and products. The catalog also offers a downloadable CSV and an email subscription.
4. Pick the last incident any of your vendors announced in the past year (search the vendor's blog or status history). Ask what the business did, how long it took, and who decided.

## Pass criteria
Every tier 1 vendor has a monitored feed or subscription and a monitored contact address, and one person is named to read them. Products you host yourself are checked against known-exploited vulnerabilities at least monthly, or are covered by scheduled scanning as in APP-009. The last vendor incident shows a written decision, even if the decision was "no action needed".

## Fix
Subscribe a shared mailbox to each critical vendor's status and security notices, and add feeds to a channel that someone watches. Register the shared address in each vendor account as the security and billing contact. Subscribe to the CISA catalog for the products you run. Add "vendor incident" as a trigger in your incident process: rotate credentials and API keys tied to that vendor, check logs for unusual access, and decide whether customers or the privacy regulator must be told (see your breach-response checks).

## Risk and rollback
Feeds create noise; filter to the vendors and products you run and review the filter every quarter. Do not point alerts at personal accounts that may be closed when someone leaves. Rollback: unsubscribe or narrow the filter, but keep at least the vendor emails and status feeds for tier 1.

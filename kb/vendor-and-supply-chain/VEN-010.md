# VEN-010 · Every critical vendor has a tested exit plan: your own data copy, an alternative, and accounts you control

## Why it matters
Vendors get breached, bought, repriced, suspended for a billing mistake, or simply shut down. If your customer records, invoices, website or domain exist only inside one vendor's account, your recovery time is whatever that vendor decides. NIST's supply chain guidance treats the ability to replace a supplier and recover as part of managing the risk. For a small business, a workable exit plan is short: know what you would lose, hold your own copy of it, and know where you would go next. This check links to the backup and recovery checks (BAK-002, BAK-005, BAK-008), which prove restores for your own systems.

## How to test
For each tier 1 vendor from VEN-001, answer in writing and check the evidence.
1. Data: what data lives only at this vendor? Can you export it yourself without vendor staff help? Do the export now, open the file, and confirm it contains the records and attachments in a usable format (CSV, JSON, SQL dump, standard file types). Note how long it took.
2. Independent copy: is a recent export or backup stored outside the vendor (BAK-002), and was a restore or import into another tool ever tried?
3. Account control: the account owner or primary admin is a role mailbox or a company-owned account with MFA, not a freelancer, agency or one employee's personal email. The business is the registered owner of the domain, DNS, hosting account, app store and payment accounts, and a second person can reach them (see DNS-001).
4. Alternative: name at least one replacement and the main work needed to move (data import, DNS change, retraining). Estimate the time and compare it with your recovery objectives (BAK-008).
5. Contract: the exit terms from VEN-003 are in place: data return window, format, deletion confirmation, and what happens on vendor insolvency or acquisition.
6. Custom software: if an outside developer built or hosts your code, you hold the source repository, build instructions and credentials, or a source escrow arrangement.
7. Tabletop: walk through "this vendor is down for a week" and "this vendor closed tonight" for the most critical one, noting what stops, what workaround exists and who decides.

## Pass criteria
Each tier 1 vendor has a one-page exit note (what is lost, your data copy and its date, the alternative, the effort, the contract terms, the contacts). A trial export has succeeded within the last 12 months. Business-owned admin accounts and domain control are confirmed. A tabletop exercise on the most critical vendor was done in the last 12 months and its gaps are on a list with owners.

## Fix
Schedule regular exports to storage you control, following your backup rules (3-2-1, encrypted, tested). Transfer ownership of accounts and domains into the business's name, with a shared role mailbox for notices. Prefer products that use open formats and have export tools. Record the exit note next to the vendor inventory row. For single-vendor dependencies you cannot change quickly, document the manual fallback process.

## Risk and rollback
Exports can contain personal information, so protect them like production data (encryption, restricted access, deletion when replaced). Changing account ownership can briefly lock people out; do it with the vendor's guided process and keep the old admin active until the new one is confirmed. Rollback: restore the previous owner contact in the vendor console, delete the trial export, and note the problem for the next review.

# CLD-007 · Audit logging is on, kept long enough, and sign-in and admin-change alerts reach a person

## Why it matters
When an account is taken over, the questions are immediate: who signed in, from where, what did they change, and are they still in? Without an audit log you cannot answer, and without an alert you learn about the problem from a customer. Some services do not record everything by default, or keep the record only briefly, and an alert that goes to an unread mailbox is the same as none.

## How to test
For each primary service confirm that logging is on, find how far back it goes, and prove that alerts arrive.
- Microsoft 365: in Exchange Online PowerShell run `Get-AdminAuditLogConfig | Format-List UnifiedAuditLogIngestionEnabled`; it must be `True`. Microsoft notes auditing is not on by default for small and medium business licences, so do not assume. Then search the audit log in the Purview portal for a recent event.
- Google Workspace: Admin console, Reporting, Audit and investigation, User log events (sign-ins, whether 2-step verification was used). Then Rules, and check the system-defined alerts for suspicious login are on and where their email notifications go. Open the Alert center and read recent alerts.
- Cloudflare: Manage Account, Audit Logs. For a longer window use the API: `curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" "https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/accounts/$ACCOUNT_ID/logs/audit?since=2026-10-01T00:00:00Z&before=2026-11-09T00:00:00Z"` (the token needs Account Settings Read).
- GitHub organisation: Settings, Logs, Audit log; search for recent member and permission changes.
- Cloud host, registrar and payment provider: find the activity or security log and check the sign-in and key-creation events are visible.
Then run an alert test: ask a second admin to sign in from a new browser, change a test user's role and remove it, and confirm the event appears in the log and that the designated recipient is told within the time you expect.

## Pass criteria
Audit logging is enabled in every service that holds admin or customer data. Records are kept at least 90 days in a place an attacker with an ordinary admin login cannot silently erase, or longer where law or contract requires (the 90 day floor is our judgement, not a standard). Alerts exist for at least these events: suspicious or new-location admin sign-in, role grants and removals, MFA or recovery changes, new API tokens, and any use of the break-glass account from CLD-002. Alerts go to a shared mailbox or phone that more than one person watches, and the test above produced a notification.

## Fix
Turn on unified auditing in Microsoft 365 (`Set-AdminAuditLogConfig -UnifiedAuditLogIngestionEnabled $true`, which can take up to an hour to apply). In Google Workspace create activity rules for the events above with the Alert center as the destination and add email recipients. Send the alert emails to a shared role mailbox that is forwarded to phones. Export logs from services with short retention to storage you control. Write down who reads an alert and what they do first (see the incident-response pillar).

## Risk and rollback
Logs hold personal data, so limit who can read them and follow your retention policy. Too many alert rules train people to ignore them; begin with the short list above. Turning auditing on does not change how users work. Rollback: disable the individual alert rule, but keep the logging itself on.

# CLD-009 · The DNS and CDN account and the domain registrar are locked down against takeover

## Why it matters
Whoever controls your DNS and CDN account controls where your domain points. An attacker with that login can redirect the website to a fake page, intercept mail by changing MX records, request certificates for your names and quietly remove your protections. The same applies to the registrar account, which can transfer the domain away altogether. These two accounts are small in number and huge in consequence, so they deserve stricter handling than ordinary SaaS logins. This check covers the account side; the DNS records themselves are covered in the dns pillar.

## How to test
Cloudflare is used as the example; apply the same questions to any other DNS, CDN or registrar provider.
1. Sign-in: My Profile, Authentication. The owner should have a security key and a second method registered, with backup codes stored offline (see CLD-001).
2. Members: Manage Account, Members. List members and roles. Cloudflare roles carry a scope, so check that day-to-day helpers (an agency or developer) have a limited role on specific zones, not full administrator on the whole account. Confirm there is more than one Super Administrator (CLD-002), and that 2FA enforcement for members is on.
3. Tokens: Manage Account, API Tokens. Every token should name one permission and one zone (for example read or edit DNS on a single zone), ideally with an expiry or IP restriction. Confirm the legacy Global API Key is not used by integrations (CLD-004).
4. Logs: Manage Account, Audit Logs. Confirm recent member, token and DNS changes are all explained.
5. Registrar lock: `whois example.com | grep -i status` (or `curl -s https://rdap.org/domain/example.com | jq '.status'`). Look for `clientTransferProhibited`, the transfer lock status described by ICANN. In Cloudflare Registrar the lock is under Manage Domains, the domain, Configuration. Confirm registrant and admin contact emails go to a business-owned mailbox.
6. Recovery loop: make sure the mailbox that can reset the registrar and DNS logins is not itself hosted on a domain that depends on that same DNS account without a second route in.

## Pass criteria
The DNS and CDN owners and the registrar account use hardware keys or passkeys with offline recovery codes. There are at least two owners, with other users limited by role and zone. All API tokens are zone- and permission-scoped, and no unexplained members, tokens or changes appear in the audit log. The domain is registrar-locked (`clientTransferProhibited` present) and contact details are current and business-owned. Automation does not rely on a global key.

## Fix
Turn on 2FA enforcement for all members, move helpers to scoped roles, delete unused members and tokens, and replace any global key with a scoped token. Turn on the registrar lock, set the registrant and technical contacts to role mailboxes, and keep the domain's renewal and payment details current. Consider extra protections your registrar offers for high-value domains (the registry-level lock described in the Cloudflare registrar documentation, where available). Add DNS and registrar changes to the alerts in CLD-007.

## Risk and rollback
The registrar lock blocks planned transfers until you unlock the domain (and Cloudflare produces a new authorisation code), so unlock only when you intend to move the domain, then re-lock immediately. Narrowing a helper's role can stop their deployments, so tell them first. Rollback: restore the earlier role or token from your notes, but prefer issuing a new scoped token to restoring a broad one.

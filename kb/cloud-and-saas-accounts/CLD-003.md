# CLD-003 · Admin rights are limited to named people with the narrowest role, and nobody shares an admin login

## Why it matters
Most small teams hand out the top role because it is the quickest way to make something work, and then never take it back. The result is many people who can delete everything, and, when they share a single "admin" login, no record of who did what and no way to remove one person without changing the password for all. If one of those accounts is phished, the attacker inherits full control. Narrow roles and personal accounts limit the damage and make the audit log meaningful.

## How to test
Produce a list of every account with elevated rights per service and compare it with what each person actually needs.
- Google Workspace: Admin console, Account, Admin roles; open the Super Admin role and each custom role to see who holds it.
- Microsoft 365: Entra admin center, Roles and administrators; open Global Administrator and any other privileged roles.
- Cloudflare: Manage Account, Members; compare the role of each member and the scope (all zones or only one).
- GitHub: `gh api 'orgs/ORG/members?role=admin' --jq '.[].login'` for owners; repository admin rights under each repository's Settings, Collaborators and teams.
- AWS: `aws iam list-entities-for-policy --policy-arn arn:aws:iam::aws:policy/AdministratorAccess` shows who holds full admin.
- Azure: `az role assignment list --all --include-inherited --query "[?roleDefinitionName=='Owner'||roleDefinitionName=='Contributor']" -o table`.
- Google Cloud: `gcloud projects get-iam-policy PROJECT_ID --flatten=bindings --filter="bindings.role:roles/owner" --format="table(bindings.members)"`.
Then look for shared logins: generic names such as `admin`, `office`, `info` or `dev` that can sign in, a password in a shared document or chat, and sign-in logs showing one account used from several places or people. Ask each admin whether they use that account for daily mail and browsing.

## Pass criteria
Every elevated account belongs to one named person (or is a documented service account or break-glass account from CLD-002). The number of full owners or super admins is small (two or three is typical for a small business) and each other admin has a role scoped to the task and, where the service allows, to the resource. Administrators use a separate account for daily work rather than the admin identity. No admin login is shared, and no shared mailbox that can receive resets has a password known to several people.

## Fix
Replace each shared login by individual accounts with the narrowest built-in role that does the job; move the shared mailbox to a delegated shared-mailbox feature of your email suite. Create separate daily-use and admin identities for people who need both. Remove roles nobody can justify, and on AWS, Azure and Google Cloud replace broad roles with the specific ones the task needs, using the provider's access-analysis tools to see what was actually used. Review the lists every six months.

## Risk and rollback
Removing a right that someone quietly depended on breaks their automation or a scheduled job, so announce the change and check scheduled tasks and integrations first. Remove the role in stages (downgrade, wait a week, remove) rather than deleting accounts. Rollback: re-assign the role from your saved export of the previous list.

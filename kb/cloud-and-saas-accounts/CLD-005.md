# CLD-005 · Leaving staff and contractors lose access to every cloud and SaaS account the same day

## Why it matters
Former employees and contractors are a common source of both malicious damage and plain accidents, and an account that nobody remembers is an easy target for attackers because no one notices it being used. Disabling the main email login is not enough: people also hold personal access tokens, are members of the code host and the DNS provider, know shared passwords, and have granted third-party apps access to company data. Same-day removal, backed by a checklist, closes these gaps while the memory of what the person had is still fresh.

## How to test
Take the last three people who left in the past 12 months (staff, contractors, agency users) and trace each one.
1. Check the written offboarding checklist exists and lists every service from the inventory in CLD-010.
2. For each person and each service confirm the account is gone or suspended and compare the date with the last working day. Google Workspace: Directory, Users, look for status Suspended or deleted; Reporting, Audit and investigation, User log events shows any sign-in after the departure date. Microsoft 365: Entra admin center, Users, the account should be blocked from sign-in with sessions revoked (the Revoke sessions button, or `Revoke-MgUserSignInSession` in Microsoft Graph PowerShell), and the sign-in log should show nothing afterwards. GitHub: organisation People, and Outside collaborators. Cloudflare: Manage Account, Members. AWS: `aws iam list-users` plus the credential report for password and key last-used dates.
3. Look for what survives the account: personal access tokens and API keys the person created (CLD-004), shared passwords they knew, forwarding rules and delegated mailbox access, OAuth grants (CLD-006), and company data owned only by their account.
4. Check that no billing, owner, security or recovery contact still points at a former person.

## Pass criteria
All three sample departures show access removed on the last working day (hours, not weeks) in every service on the inventory, with no later sign-in activity. A checklist is used each time. Sessions are revoked, not only the password changed, and secrets the person knew or created were rotated. Data and ownership were transferred to a named person before deletion, and contact fields no longer name the former person.

## Fix
Write a one-page checklist built from the inventory and name who runs it and who backs them up. Do the identity step first (suspend or block sign-in and revoke sessions), then services outside single sign-on, then secrets. Where possible use single sign-on and automatic deprovisioning so that one action closes most accounts. Suspend before deleting so data can still be transferred; Google documents that unmoved data can be lost on deletion, and suspended accounts there are still billed until deleted. Schedule a check at the next review that no departed person's account remains.

## Risk and rollback
Cutting access abruptly can interrupt mail flow, scheduled jobs and customer-facing work the person ran, so assign a successor and set a mail forward or delegation before the cut. Suspension is reversible; deletion at many providers is not, or only for a short window, so suspend first and delete later. Rollback: re-enable the suspended account if removal was a mistake.

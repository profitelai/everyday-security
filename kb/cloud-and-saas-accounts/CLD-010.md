# CLD-010 · A short inventory lists every SaaS and cloud account with a named owner

## Why it matters
Every other check in this pillar depends on knowing what accounts exist. Small businesses accumulate dozens of services: trial accounts that turned into real ones, an old analytics login, a forgotten test server still holding customer data and still billing. An account nobody owns is never updated, never offboarded and never noticed when it is breached. A one-page inventory with one accountable person per line is the cheapest control in the pillar.

## How to test
Read the inventory, then test whether it is complete by comparing it with independent evidence.
1. Check the inventory has, for each account: service and purpose, named owner and deputy, who holds admin rights, sign-in method (single sign-on or password, MFA status), billing contact and payment method, kind of data stored (customer or personal data, yes or no), and the date it was last reviewed.
2. Look for services that are missing from it, using at least these sources:
   - Subscription lines on the company card and bank statements, and invoices in the accounting mailbox.
   - Welcome, verification and receipt emails in shared mailboxes.
   - The list of connected apps in Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra or GitHub (CLD-006).
   - DNS: `dig +short TXT example.com` often shows site-verification tokens that reveal which services were set up for the domain; check MX, SPF includes and CNAME records for email and web services.
   - The password manager's list of entries.
3. For a sample of five entries, ask the named owner to confirm they are still the owner and can reach the admin console.
4. Check that closed or unused accounts were actually cancelled, not just forgotten.

## Pass criteria
Every service found in step 2 appears in the inventory with a named owner who agrees. The inventory is kept in a place the whole leadership team can open, even if the owner is away, and is reviewed at least once a year and whenever a service is added or a person leaves. Orphaned or unknown accounts found during the review are closed or assigned an owner. Data classification is recorded so that services that hold personal information can be matched with your privacy obligations.

## Fix
Start a simple table (a spreadsheet is enough) and fill it from the sources in step 2. Assign an owner and a deputy to every line, close what is not needed, and add a "new service" step to purchasing: no card payment or sign-up for a business tool without an inventory line. Feed the inventory into the offboarding checklist (CLD-005). Where a service stores personal data, link it to your record of service providers in the privacy-compliance pillar.

## Risk and rollback
The inventory contains a map of your systems, so keep it where only staff with a need can read it and never put passwords or keys in it. Closing an account you thought unused may break an integration or lose data, so export the data and wait a short period before final deletion. Rollback: reopen or restore the service from the export, and update the inventory line.

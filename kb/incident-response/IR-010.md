# IR-010 · A one-page 'first hour' sheet is printed, current and used by whoever answers first

## Why it matters
The first hour decides how much damage is done and how much evidence survives. The person who first sees a problem is rarely the most experienced, and under stress people skip steps. A single page that says what to do, in what order, and what not to do (do not wipe, do not reply to the attacker, do not tell the whole company by mail) keeps the response calm. It condenses the larger plan: it is the page that stays on the wall.

## How to test
1. Locate the sheet. It should be one page, readable in two minutes, printed, and also available on a phone.
2. Check that it contains, in order:
   - Stay calm; write down the time and what you saw; do not delete, reboot or wipe anything yet.
   - Call the incident lead (number on the sheet); if there is no answer in 10 minutes, call the deputy.
   - Classify using the severity scale (IR-004).
   - Contain: disconnect the affected laptop from network, disable the suspect account, rotate the exposed password from a clean device, block the IP or suspend the sending queue as the plan says (IR-005).
   - Preserve: take a snapshot or screenshot, export the relevant logs, note who did what (IR-005, IR-006).
   - Call the technical responder and the host or provider if their service is involved (IR-003).
   - Ask the privacy question: is personal information involved? If yes, bring in the privacy lead and start the incident record (IR-007, PRIV-006).
   - Do not: agree to pay or negotiate with an extortionist (that decision belongs to the owner with advice, not to the first responder), contact the attacker, post publicly, or contact customers before the communications owner agrees.
   - Report: police for crime in progress or extortion, and optionally the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security (IR-007).
   - Hand over: at the end of the hour, the lead writes a status line (what is known, what is done, what is next).
3. Check dates, names and phone numbers against IR-002 and IR-003.
4. Use it in the next tabletop (IR-009): hand it to a person who has never seen it and watch whether they follow it.

## Pass criteria
A one-page sheet exists, is dated within the last 6 to 12 months, covers the steps above including the "do not" list, matches the current plan and contact list, is posted in at least one physical location and one digital location that does not depend on the systems under attack, and a new employee can use it without extra explanation.

## Fix
Make the sheet from the plan, keep it to one side of a page, use large type, and print it. Add a QR code to the offline copy of the full plan only if it opens without your own infrastructure. Review it whenever a name, number or provider changes, and after each exercise or incident.

## Risk and rollback
An out-of-date sheet is worse than none because it sends people to the wrong number. Put the revision date on the page and replace printed copies when you update it. Rollback: if a step on the sheet proves harmful in a test, remove or reword it and recirculate the page the same day.

# IR-003 · A current incident contact list covers internal staff, providers, advisers and authorities, and is kept off the systems it protects

## Why it matters
During an incident you need phone numbers and account references in minutes: the hosting provider's abuse or emergency line, the domain registrar, the mail or DNS provider, your cloud vendors, whoever manages backups, your insurer, a lawyer, and the police. Looking them up through a compromised mailbox or a site that is down wastes the first hour. Guidance on response plans asks for alternate contact methods and a backup contact for every team member, because the usual channel may be the one that is broken.

## How to test
1. Obtain the contact list. Check that it contains, for each entry, a name, a phone number that is answered out of hours, an alternate channel, and the account or contract reference you will be asked for.
2. Verify coverage against this list: owner and deputy; technical responder; hosting or server provider; domain registrar; DNS provider; mail provider or mail-server admin; each cloud and SaaS vendor that holds customer data; payment processor; backup provider; cyber insurance broker (if any); lawyer; privacy lead; police non-emergency line; the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security reporting form; the privacy regulators (Commission d'acces a l'information du Quebec, Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada) as pointers.
3. Dial three entries at random, including one provider's emergency line, and record whether the number works and who answers.
4. Find out where copies are stored (see IR-001) and the date of last update.

## Pass criteria
All the categories above are present, each with an out-of-hours contact and an alternate channel; at least one copy is reachable without your own mail, SSO or website; three random test calls succeeded; the list was checked within the last six months and after any change of provider or staff.

## Fix
Build the list in a shared document with an offline copy, and add the entry "who can prove ownership to the registrar and host" for each account. Review it on a calendar reminder every quarter and whenever someone leaves. Agree out-of-hours support terms with providers before you need them.

## Risk and rollback
The list holds sensitive phone numbers and account references, so restrict who can read it, and keep passwords out of it. Rollback: if a copy leaks, change the affected provider PINs and security questions and publish a new version.

# IR-007 · Communication owners, holding statements and a regulator-notification pointer sheet exist before an incident

## Why it matters
What you say, to whom and when is part of the response. Customers, staff, the host, your insurer and regulators may all need to hear from you, and contradictory or panicked messages cause more harm than the incident itself. Canadian privacy law adds deadlines and content rules when personal information is involved. This check covers the communication workflow only; the detailed legal decision procedure and register are in PRIV-006 and PRIV-007. Under PIPEDA, an organisation must report to the federal Commissioner and notify affected people when a breach of security safeguards creates a real risk of significant harm, as soon as feasible after it determines the breach occurred, and must keep a record of every breach for 24 months. Under Quebec's private-sector law, an incident presenting a risk of serious injury must be notified promptly to the Commission d'acces a l'information and to the people concerned, and a register of incidents must be kept. Which regime applies depends on where you operate and where your customers live. This is operational guidance, not legal advice.

## How to test
1. Find the pointer sheet: a one-page document with who decides whether to notify, the question to ask first ("is personal information involved, whose, and from which province?"), links to the OPC report form and risk-of-harm assessment tool, the Commission d'acces a l'information incident notice page, the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security reporting form, and your lawyer's number.
2. Check that a single spokesperson and a deputy are named, and that nobody else is to speak publicly or to the press.
3. Read the template statements: an internal staff notice, a short holding statement for customers ("we are investigating an issue affecting X; here is how to reach us"), a full notice to affected people with the content items the law requires (see S-QC-INCREG and the OPC mandatory reporting page), and a note for providers and insurers.
4. Check how customers will be reached if mail is down (a status page on a different domain, SMS, a phone message), and that nobody promises facts that are not yet known.
5. Ask whether the business notes the date it determined a breach occurred, since the clocks run from that decision, and whether a decision memo records the risk reasoning either way.

## Pass criteria
A named communications owner and deputy; written templates for staff, customers, regulators and providers; an alternate channel not dependent on the affected systems; a pointer sheet with the regulators' current notification routes; a rule to record the decision date and reasoning in the register (PRIV-006). Legal review of the templates took place at least once.

## Fix
Draft the holding statement and the two notices now, in English and French when you serve Quebec. Prepare the pointer sheet from the regulators' own pages and put it in the plan (IR-001). Agree with your lawyer which incidents trigger a call to them. Set up a simple status page or social account on separate infrastructure.

## Risk and rollback
Sending a notice too early can spread unverified claims; sending it too late can breach statutory duties. Keep wording factual, avoid speculating about cause, and correct any error with a dated follow-up. Rollback: if a notice was inaccurate, issue a correction rather than deleting the original.

# IR-009 · A tabletop exercise is run at least once a year and its findings change the plan

## Why it matters
A plan that has never been walked through usually fails in small ways: the contact is out of date, nobody has the registrar login, the lead does not know they may shut a system down. A tabletop is a facilitated discussion around a realistic scenario, with no systems touched. It is cheap, takes one to two hours and finds most of these gaps. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security recommends exercises and an annual revision of the plan, and CISA publishes free tabletop scenario packages that can be adapted.

## How to test
1. Ask for the record of the last exercise: date, scenario, participants, findings, and actions.
2. Check that participants included the owner or decision maker, the technical responder, the communications owner, the privacy lead, and at least one non-technical employee.
3. Check the scenarios rotate across the likely risks for your business: compromised mailbox leading to invoice fraud, ransomware on the file server, web shell on a customer-facing site, lost laptop with customer data, a cloud account takeover, a provider outage, leaked database dump.
4. Check the exercise timed how long key steps took (reach the lead, decide to contain, draft the regulator and customer notices) and compared them with the plan's targets.
5. Confirm that actions from the exercise were assigned, completed and reflected in the plan version history.

## Pass criteria
An exercise took place within the last 12 months, included decision makers and a non-technical participant, produced a written findings list, and each finding has an owner and a completion date or was explicitly accepted. The plan version history shows changes made as a result.

## Fix
Use a ready-made package (for example a CISA tabletop package) and a 60 to 90 minute slot. Choose one person to facilitate and one to take notes. Read a scenario in steps, adding new facts as it unfolds, and ask each time "who does what, with which access, in how many minutes?" End with a list of fixes. Do a short repeat (30 minutes) after major changes such as a new provider or new staff in key roles.

## Risk and rollback
There is almost no technical risk. The practical risk is exposing sensitive details in the notes or scaring staff; keep the notes private to the incident team and present the exercise as a test of the plan, not of people. Rollback: not applicable; if a session goes badly, rerun it with a simpler scenario.

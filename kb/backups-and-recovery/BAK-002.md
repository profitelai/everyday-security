# BAK-002 · Important data exists in at least three copies, on two kinds of storage, with one off-site

## Why it matters
Each part of the familiar "3-2-1" rule covers a different failure. Keeping several copies survives the loss of any one; using two kinds of storage avoids a single hardware or vendor fault taking everything; one copy in another location survives fire, theft, a provider closing the account, or a whole site being compromised. A second disk in the same server fails the last test.

## How to test
Make a table for each system that holds important data and fill in these rows by looking at the real configuration, not at what was intended:
1. Copy one is the live data. Where is it?
2. Where is each backup copy, and on what kind of storage (local disk, another server, object storage, tape or removable disk)?
3. Which copy is in a different physical location and under a different account from the live system?
4. Which copy would survive if the live host, its cloud account and its hosting provider were all lost?

## Pass criteria
There are at least three copies in total (the live data and two backups), spread over two different kinds or providers of storage, and at least one backup is held at a different site and under separate credentials from production. The table is written down and has a review date.

## Fix
Add the missing leg. A common small-business shape is a local or same-region backup for fast restores plus a second copy in object storage with another provider, or a pulled copy on a machine at another location. Review the table when systems are added or moved.

## Risk and rollback
Extra copies cost money and widen the set of places that hold your data, so apply the same access control and encryption to every copy (see BAK-003). Rollback: retire a redundant copy only after a restore from the remaining ones has been tested.
